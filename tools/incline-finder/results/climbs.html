Mölndal · OpenStreetMap + 2 elevation models

A kilometre at five per cent

Every road within 4 km of Eklanda Hage, walked uphill and measured against two independent elevation models. Five climbs come close to the target; one is almost exactly it.

Closest to the brief

Sisjövägen — 995 m at 5.0 %, 50 m of climbing, on one road with no turns, 2.0 km from your door.

From the Datavägen/Knapegårdsvägen junction at 14 m, south-east and up to 65 m. Both elevation models agree to within 0.25 percentage points. A parallel path runs alongside the full length, though OpenStreetMap tags it as path/footway rather than a cycleway — worth a look before you commit to repeats.

Where they are

Rings at 1, 2 and 3 km from Eklanda Hage. Nothing inside 2 km holds a steady 5 % for a kilometre — the closest candidate, the service road up Västra Eklanda Höjd at 625 m, is steeper in one model than the other and flattens in its middle third.

How this was measured

Roads

Overture Maps transportation segments (OpenStreetMap-derived), July 2026 release — 13,075 tarmac segments within 4 km, resampled every 25 m.

Elevation

AWS Terrain Tiles for the search, Copernicus GLO-30 as an independent check. Both are surface models: trees and buildings beside the road add noise, so profiles are smoothed over 60 m.

Search

A depth-first walk uphill from every junction, dropping any branch that gives back more than 6 m below its high point, then trimming each corridor to the stretch that best matches 1 km at 5 %.

What to trust

Averages over a kilometre are solid to about ±0.3 points. Individual 100 m readings in the strip are not — treat the bands as shape, not gospel.
